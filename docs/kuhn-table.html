1 · Kuhn poker in one minute

Poker with three cards and one round of betting. Small enough to draw the whole game, but it still has hidden cards and bluffing, which is what CFR is built for.

How a hand goes

  1. Both players put 1 chip in the pot. This is the ante.
  2. The deck is J, Q, K (J lowest). Each player gets one card face down. The third card is unused. You see only your own card.
  3. You (player 0) act first: check (put nothing in) or bet 1 chip.
  4. If you checked, player 1 checks (go to showdown) or bets 1. If player 1 bets, you call (put in 1, go to showdown) or fold (give up the pot).
  5. If you bet, player 1 calls (showdown) or folds.
  6. Showdown: the higher card takes the pot.

Every way a hand ends, and what you win

BettingEnds inYour payoff
check, checkshowdown±1
check, bet, foldyou fold−1
check, bet, callshowdown±2
bet, foldplayer 1 folds+1
bet, callshowdown±2

Payoff is chips won minus chips put in. ± is + if your card is higher, − if lower. Folding loses only your ante; calling a bet risks 2.

3 · CFR in this notation

CFR runs a separate regret-matching learner at every decision point a player can recognize. Below are the quantities it uses, with the names this repo uses. Payoffs are always from player i's point of view.

Histories and information sets

A history h is everything that has happened, including hidden cards: one node of the tree. An information set I is a group of histories that look identical to the player acting. In the tree, it's a dashed box.

Policy vs reach

sigma(t, i, I, a) is the probability player i picks action a at information set I, under the policy at iteration t. It covers one decision and is what CFR learns.

reach(t, h) is the probability the game gets to history h at all: multiply every probability along the path, including chance's deal. Split those factors by who chose them:

reach(t, h) = reach_self(t, i, h) * reach_opp(t, i, h)
reach_self   player i's own action probabilities on the path
reach_opp    the opponent's AND chance's probabilities

Values

q(t, i, h, a)  expected payoff to i after taking a at h,
               then everyone plays the current policy

Counterfactual values and regret

cfv(t, i, I, a) = sum over h in I of reach_opp(t, i, h) * q(t, i, h, a)
cfv(t, i, I)    = sum over a of sigma(t, i, I, a) * cfv(t, i, I, a)
r(t, i, I, a)   = cfv(t, i, I, a) - cfv(t, i, I)

Weight each hidden possibility by how likely chance and the opponent made it, leave out your own reach ("suppose I played to get here"), and don't normalize.

CFR+ update

R(t, i, I, a)       = max(R(t-1, i, I, a) + r(t, i, I, a), 0)
sigma(t+1, i, I, ·) = regret_matching(R(t, i, I, ·))

Regret matching: play each action in proportion to its positive regret; if all are zero, play uniformly.

Average policy (the one that converges)

avg(i, I, a) ∝ sum over t of  w_t * reach_self(t, i, I) * sigma(t, i, I, a)
w_t = t ** avg_power

The proportionality constant is the matching sum without the σ factor. Regrets use reach_opp; the average uses reach_self.

4 · Exercise: one CFR+ update by hand

You are player 0 (i = 0) at iteration t = 2 of CFR+. Compute the update at a single information set, using the bot's strategy above as player 1's current policy.

The information set

I = "I hold Q, I checked, player 1 bet." Your actions are call or fold. It contains two histories, both inside the orange box in the tree when you hold Q:

  • h_J: deal (you Q, bot J), you check, bot bets
  • h_K: deal (you Q, bot K), you check, bot bets

After you call or fold, the hand ends, so q(t, 0, h, a) is just the payoff from the table in section 1.

Bot bets after your check, holding K1

Your accumulated regret R(t−1) at Icall 0, fold 0.1

Q1Compute reach_opp(t, 0, h) for both histories. Which factors on each path belong to chance and the bot, and which to you?
Hint

Walk the path to each history and list every probability on it. Your own check (0.8) is on the path too. Does it belong in reach_opp?

Q2Compute cfv(t, 0, I, call), cfv(t, 0, I, fold), and cfv(t, 0, I).
Hint

For each action, the sum has one term per history in I: that history's reach_opp times your payoff if the hand ended with that action. Don't divide by the total weight.

Q3Compute the instantaneous regrets r(t, 0, I, call) and r(t, 0, I, fold). What do they tell you about this spot?
Q4Apply the CFR+ update to get R(t, 0, I, ·), then regret matching to get next iteration's policy sigma(t+1, 0, I, ·).
Q5This iteration's contribution to the average policy at I: the numerator term w_t * reach_self * sigma for each action, and the denominator term w_t * reach_self.
Hint

reach_self is the product of only your own action probabilities on the path to I. In both histories, what did you do before reaching I?
