Regret matching

A rule for choosing your next policy from your record so far: keep a running score per action, and play each action in proportion to how good its score looks.

The rule

For each action, keep a running total called its regret: how much more you would have won if you had always played that action instead of what you actually played.

  • Update the tally. After each round, add q(a) − v to each action's tally. q(a) is what action a would have earned this round; v is what you actually earned in expectation. This q − v is the same advantage as in policy gradient.
  • Next policy. Play each action in proportion to its positive tally. Actions whose tally is zero or below get probability 0. If no action has a positive tally, play uniformly.
R(a) += q(a) - v                  # accumulate regret
sigma(a) = max(R(a), 0) / sum_b max(R(b), 0)   # uniform if the sum is 0

In this repo, regret_matching(regrets, legal) in rlgames/core/cfr.py is the second line. The tally itself lives in CFR.

Example: RPS against an opponent who keeps playing rock

Payoffs are win +1, tie 0, loss −1. Actions are ordered (R, P, S).

roundyour policyq (R, P, S)vtally after (R, P, S)
1uniform(0, +1, −1)0(0, +1, −1)
2all paper(0, +1, −1)+1(−1, +1, −3)

After round 1, only paper has a positive tally, so you switch to all paper. In round 2 you are winning, so every alternative gains negative regret and you stay on paper.

What it is not

  • Not a best response. It does not jump to whatever beats the opponent's latest move. Every past round counts equally in the tally, so one surprise does not erase your history.
  • Not a gradient step. There is no learning rate and no parameters. The policy is recomputed from the totals each round. Policy gradient nudges logits by π·(q − v); regret matching sets σ ∝ max(R, 0) directly.
  • The current policy is not what converges. In self-play the current policy can keep cycling. The guarantee is that regret grows slowly (at most about √T), and by the folk theorem that makes the average policy converge to an equilibrium.

Not optimized directly. Policy gradient climbs a fixed function J(θ) = Σ π(a) q(a) you can evaluate now. Regret, max_a Σ_t q_t(a) − Σ_t v_t, depends on the whole history and on opponent moves you have not seen yet, so there is nothing to evaluate and push down this step. Regret matching is instead a rule with a worst-case guarantee: whatever sequence of qs arrives, regret stays sublinear.
